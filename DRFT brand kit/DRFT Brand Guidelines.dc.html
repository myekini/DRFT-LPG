01 — THE IDEA
Two states of one letter. The symbol is a “d” mid-edit: its stem has stepped away and become an insertion cursor. The wordmark is the same “d” after approval: whole, settled, in ink.
PROPOSED
The symbol. Used alone only where a wordmark cannot fit: favicon, app icon, avatar.
APPROVED
The wordmark’s “d”. The primary logo is always the full wordmark, never the symbol.
02 — SYMBOL
Built on a 150-unit height. Bowl stroke 24, cursor stroke 28, gap 24. The cursor is heavier than the bowl so it reads as a cursor and not a second letter. Never close the gap; the settled form is the wordmark’s job.

03 — WORDMARK
Custom-drawn, monolinear, 18-unit stroke on the same 150-unit grid. Square terminals throughout. The “r” keeps its downward tick, the flag of an editing cursor. Always lowercase. Never set the name in a typeface.
04 — LOCKUPS, CLEAR SPACE, MINIMUM SIZE
The symbol is never placed beside the wordmark; it would read “d drft”. In the horizontal lockup the mark is the initial: the mid-edit “d” followed by r, f, t in ink. The stacked lockup puts the symbol above the tagline.

Clear space on every asset is 1× the x-height (the bowl height) on all sides.

Minimum sizes: wordmark 64px wide / 16mm print. Symbol 16px / 5mm. Horizontal lockup 96px wide.

05 — COLOR
Paper and ink carry the brand. Mint appears in one role: a change that is proposed, active, or just confirmed. It never fills a background, never glows, never becomes a gradient. On light surfaces use Mint Deep for anything that must be read; Mint is for dark surfaces and small fills.
